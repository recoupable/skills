# Generate the media, then use it

The agent must execute the asset production plan, not only write prompts. For a new mixed-media launch film, create original image and video sources where the brief needs them. Approved existing assets may be retained; a targeted text fix should not regenerate accepted media. Record any waived generation stage and why. If required generation is unavailable, deliver a clearly labeled partial draft rather than quietly substituting placeholder art.

## Discover capabilities and plan shots

Inspect the available image and video tools and their current schemas. Use the host's image-generation tool for original stills/edits and an available video-generation connector such as Higgsfield for moving scenes. Read applicable tool/skill instructions. Do not hard-code model names, prices or credentials from earlier projects. A link to a provider is not access to it; report a missing connection accurately.

For each asset record: narrative job, medium, subject/identity references, opening state, visible action, landing, camera, lighting/materials, aspect ratio, duration, edit handles and intended crop. Plan distinct concepts and behaviors rather than a wall of recolored portraits. Select sources that can survive the final composition. Keep exact logos and typography authored separately.

Use authorized artist references and music. Choose the soundtrack passage before editing. Explain additional quoted spending only when new authorization is required; preserve the user's existing scope/budget and do not ask again for already authorized generations. Tool/provider approval rules still apply. Log estimates separately from confirmed charges.

## Generate, inspect, select

1. Produce image candidates for identity, cover/poster art, environments or video first frames. Inspect actual returned images at meaningful resolution before using them as video references. An attractive portrait is not automatically a useful action shot.
2. Generate moving clips with specific subject action, camera behavior and usable entry/exit states. Use image-to-video when visual continuity matters, or another supported mode suited to the shot. Do not call zooming a still generated performance footage.
3. Save the tool/model, prompt, reference IDs/hashes, settings, job ID, submission time, quoted cost and status. Resume/poll the existing job; never resubmit an uncertain request just because it is slow. Respect polling guidance and maintain progress updates.
4. Retrieve completed outputs through the provider's supported delivery mechanism. Inspect start, action and landing, consecutive frames at difficult motion, and the intended crop. Review identity, anatomy, temporal coherence and visible action. Mark rejected takes with hashes and reasons. Generation success alone is not creative acceptance.
5. If the selected source is weak, revise its concrete shot direction and generate a replacement within authorization. Do not hide a failed source behind grain, UI, fast cuts or constant outer camera moves.
6. Integrate selected files into the actual final composition, with manifest references, hashes, trims and source timestamps. Keep complete useful actions and explicit source clocks. Render and review the composite; a source can work in isolation and fail when cropped or obscured.

## Generation and delivery evidence

The asset ledger should distinguish requested, running, failed, complete-unreviewed, rejected and selected. Preserve selected and rejected receipts privately. Record where each selected asset appears in the film, and which assets also have standalone exports. Do not claim dozens of unique generations when the film displays layout variants of a few sources.

A cinematic teaser can combine several generated shots; lyrics, album art, posters and visualizers may combine generated imagery with deterministic graphics. Keep lyrics tied to verified text/timestamps, and audio-reactive graphics tied to the real soundtrack. Do not claim lip-sync, listening review or production product output without evidence.
