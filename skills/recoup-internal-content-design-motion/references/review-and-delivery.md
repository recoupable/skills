# Review and delivery

Keep one durable project record beside editable source. Record supported product claims; current brand sources; reference observations; shot jobs; generation job IDs/status, quoted cost and actual cost when available; asset hashes and selection/rejection reasons; edit/cue recipes; export metadata and hash; review ranges and limitations. Reuse the project's existing manifest rather than adding a competing source of truth. Resume known jobs instead of repeating paid generation.

Review source-only media and the final composite separately. Check opening/file drop, request/send, output reveal, transitions, type holds, CTA and any loop seam. Inspect consecutive decoded frames around changes and native-resolution crops for faces, lettering, masks and occlusion. Contact sheets establish composition coverage, not smooth playback or audio synchronization.

Play and listen to the actual export when the available tools support perception. Report unavailable listening/playback honestly; a working play button is not evidence of perceived quality. Verify dimensions, duration, frame rate, audio presence and decode integrity. Recheck affected passages after edits; changed assets or timings invalidate dependent reviews.

Track distinct states: technical validation, visual inspection, perceptual playback, audio listening, human approval and publication. Preserve the approved artifact, source recipe, dependencies and hash. Concept approval does not approve weak media. A renderer pass or completed generation does not grant human approval.

Provide playable export and editable source, with assets or a dependency manifest sufficient to recover them. Count actual independently exported assets separately from layout variants and illustrations embedded only in the film. State when the film demonstrates a future/concept workflow rather than live product output. Keep private evidence and artist assets out of this public skill. Publication remains a separate authorized action.
