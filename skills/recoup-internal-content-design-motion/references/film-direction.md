# Film direction

## Story and focal attention

Show input → request → consequence using a persistent object or identity. A generated concept or designed interface is not a recording of shipped product behavior. Verify each required action and automatic outcome; do not insert a second email form when the verified preceding action already provides that result.

Place controls over the relevant footage when that concentrates attention. A bright card beside an equally dominant moving image often creates two competing reads. Dim or soften only the supporting background while preserving crisp type and control geometry. Side-by-side layouts remain appropriate for actual comparisons.

End with an action, its object and a verified destination. “Create your release pack” communicates more than an abstract identity slogan. Give the final words and URL a stable readable hold; decorative arrows must earn their place.

## Feature identity

Treat the feature name as a designed text mark with intentional weight, spacing, proportions and silhouette within the current parent brand. Preserve supplied logo geometry. Inspect several relevant title treatments for scale, parent-brand relationship and entrance behavior; borrow mechanisms, not another brand's logo or colors. Keep the feature identity and CTA separate in purpose. Save the approved vector or lettering recipe for reuse.

## Media quality and creative breadth

Direct each source with a job, focal subject, world, visible event, viewpoint and editorial landing. “Edgy” is not a generation prompt: translate it into specific lighting, material, staging, action and framing. Use relevant identity references when authorized. Inspect complete actions and delivery crops before selecting takes; preserve rejected hashes and reasons.

A release-pack demonstration can include performance footage, animation, kinetic lyrics, cover art, posters, a carousel/mood board, a multi-shot teaser and an audio-reactive visualizer. Only claim actual exports as deliverables. Do not count resizing or color variants as wholly new generated assets. Source movement matters: camera travel over a static portrait cannot supply a performance or animation.

## Pace and concurrency

Choose the musical passage first. Measure signal data for visualization, but listen before claiming the hook, beat alignment or mix works. Faster pace means more meaningful change, not merely shorter runtime. Keep workflow copy minimal. Use overlapping creation, changes in scale and depth, and selected full-screen payoffs while maintaining one dominant subject.

Keep each moving asset's source clock continuous when its card or camera moves. Preserve enough of an action to understand it before changing the treatment. Alternate density and focus; identical equal-weight grids flatten hierarchy. Avoid repeated category titles and long serial tours when the brief asks for simultaneous abundance.

Use typography with a narrative job. Crossfading full sentences on one baseline creates double exposures; use exclusive visibility or separate geometry. Inspect the actual encoded boundary frames. Short cuts, springs and noise are choices, not automatic quality improvements.

## Reference comparison

Compare matching passages and questions: setup length, meaningful change, source action, creative variety, hierarchy, text burden, spatial continuity and sound. Record exact export hashes and reviewed time ranges. Do not invent criteria the reference itself does not meet. A clean defect list cannot override explicit user feedback that the piece feels slow or weak. State remaining reference advantages honestly without treating subjective scores as evidence of parity.
