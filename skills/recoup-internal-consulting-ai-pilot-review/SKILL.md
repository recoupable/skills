# AI pilot review

Evaluate the work through acceptance. Produce a decision record that separates learning, usable capacity, financial effects, and unproven assumptions.

## Inputs and workspace

Use the selected authorized business workspace and its instructions. Keep client data there. Bundled templates are relative to this installed skill. Read the agreed project brief, baseline, run records, costs, accepted-output checks, user feedback, and any approved measurement plan. Identify missing inputs without searching unrelated private accounts or checkouts.

If preparing a measurement plan before a pilot, complete the intended method and leave outcomes explicitly unmeasured. If reviewing a running pilot, compare against criteria agreed before the run. Report changed criteria as changes; do not quietly lower a failed target.

## Choose evidence that answers the question

- **Feasibility:** representative and difficult cases, accepted outputs, correction categories, failures, and unresolved uncertainty. State what the evidence permits the team to try next.
- **Capacity:** comparable task mix and volume; total human preparation, execution, review, and correction time; acceptance quality; actual usage; and where recovered capacity went.
- **Commercial:** qualified demand, conversion and paid outcomes, contribution after delivery costs, lag, and the preplanned comparison. Record concurrent changes in demand, pricing, staffing, or sales effort.

Keep adoption separate from correctness. A deployed system can be unused; a popular system can produce poor outputs. Count eligible tasks as well as completed runs, including failures and abandoned attempts. Explain selection bias when only successful examples were supplied.

## Reconcile the numbers

Use `templates/pilot-review.md`. Define the unit of work, period, currency, and cost basis before calculating:

- Recovered hours = matched task volume × (baseline human minutes per task − pilot human minutes per task) ÷ 60. Include preparation, checking, and corrections; preserve a negative result when work got slower.
- Capacity value = recovered hours × the stated loaded hourly cost. Label it an estimated value of time; it is not cash saved.
- Cash benefit = a separately evidenced avoided or reduced expense confirmed by its budget owner for the same period. Unchanged payroll is not a cash reduction.
- Net cash effect = confirmed cash benefits + attributable incremental contribution − incremental cash costs in the same period. Do not add capacity value again.
- ROI, if appropriate = (benefit − full incremental cost) ÷ full incremental cost, with a stated horizon and benefit definition. If the denominator is zero or unknown, report ROI as undefined, not infinite or zero. If using capacity value, label the result a capacity-value scenario.

Separate one-time setup from recurring cost. Include internal implementation time, training, licenses, infrastructure, review, repairs, and maintenance without counting the same cost twice. Show ranges for assumptions. Do not annualize a short observation as a measured annual result; a forecast must disclose its horizon, sample, volume assumption, and uncertainty.

For a comparison without random assignment, explain task mix, seasonality, selection, and other changes that could account for the result. Do not call correlation attributable revenue. Explain the limits of small samples; require enough evidence for the decision being made rather than a universal sample threshold.

## Recommend the next commitment

Compare results with the original success measure and quality floor. Recommend continued scope, expansion, a specific revision and retest, or stopping. Name the evidence and unresolved items behind the recommendation. When evidence is insufficient, propose the smallest additional observation, with an owner and a review date.

A revision needs a named failure, proposed fix, bounded effort, and another decision date. Expansion needs evidence that the next team can access, operate, review, and support the workflow. Never infer permission to deploy or reduce review from a favorable result.

Save a dated review in the entity's existing delivery folder. Keep observed, estimated, and projected numbers visibly distinct. Include baseline version, observation period, comparison limitations, cost treatment, quality findings, intended-user evidence, decision owner, and next review. Do not send the review externally unless authorized.
