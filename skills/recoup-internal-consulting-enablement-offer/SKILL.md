# Post-Discovery Enablement Offer

## Contract

Produce a reviewable offer that improves the client's team's ability to do real work with AI, whether or not they buy implementation. Scope the training purchase separately from any subsequent build. Return the offer, a delivery plan, and the commercial/evidence gaps that still need resolution; do not send, book, invoice, or change CRM state merely because a draft exists.

**Workspace:** use the explicitly selected private business workspace and its `AGENTS.md`. Read the deal/client's instructions and existing discovery/proposal records. Preserve its folder conventions; bundled paths are relative to this skill, never output destinations. Keep client facts and generated artifacts out of the public Skills repository. Ask for the workspace only if context does not identify it; do not search other private checkouts for missing evidence.

## Workflow

### 1. Read discovery and decide whether this offer fits

Read the supplied transcript/notes, current analysis, relevant communications, and approved commercial policies available in the selected workspace. Record each consequential claim with a source location and date. Separate buyer statements, observed behavior, operator interpretation, and unknowns. A summary cannot fill gaps in an unavailable transcript.

If enablement has already been delivered, read its approved scope and session records, then continue at steps 5–6; do not sell a new entry package.

Identify the sponsor, participating departments and actual end users, repeated work, current tools, desired outcomes, adoption barriers, purchasing authority, budget evidence, and timing. Do not equate an owner's enthusiasm with team demand or attendance commitments.

Choose and record one disposition:

- **Enablement offer:** the buyer wants practical team capability, or is hesitant to buy a standalone diagnostic and would value training independently. Confirm that real users can participate and bring representative work.
- **Conditional draft:** this route is plausible but essential scope/commercial facts are missing. Draft what evidence supports and list the smallest set of questions needed to complete it. Do not label it ready for delivery.
- **Keep the existing route:** an audit is already working/selected, the buyer needs specialist diagnosis, or an agreed implementation is the immediate priority. Respect that choice. A training layer may accompany a build without selling a separate entry package. Return the fit rationale and existing-engagement handoff, then stop this offer-creation workflow.

For source framing or requests to eliminate audits, read `references/source-notes.md`. End this step with a fit rationale tied to this buyer, not the article's headline. If no discovery evidence exists, produce a blank brief and targeted questions rather than inventing a client offer.

### 2. Define a bounded, standalone purchase

Use `templates/offer.md`. Recommend one coherent package, with alternatives only when the buyer's decision requires them. Do not force generic three-tier anchoring onto a narrow training purchase.

For each included item specify the audience, session count and length, delivery mode, artifact, accountable owner, and completion evidence. State the calendar window, preparation, supported tools, participant/department limits, follow-up support window, and exclusions. Treat suggested scope and dates as proposed until confirmed; do not imply reserved capacity.

Include a leadership alignment session and role-specific practice with representative work. Each hands-on session captures recurring tasks and barriers as well as teaching the team. Identify a useful first-session output and how participants will later demonstrate they can repeat the task without the consultant. Scope any recordings, reusable lessons, or SOPs explicitly; do not promise a full course by default.

State the client's preparation and access responsibilities. Choose approved tools and permissible example data before practice. If real data cannot be used, use sanitized or synthetic samples and mark any resulting evidence limits. Recording is optional and requires the client's agreement.

Completion means demonstrated capability and delivered materials, not attendance alone. Set buyer-specific acceptance criteria: task, baseline if available, follow-up check, quality standard, owner, and measurement date. Mark proposed targets as targets, never guaranteed savings or adoption.

### 3. Make the investment concrete

Consult approved pricing/payment records already available in the workspace. Explain the fee in terms of the agreed team outcomes and bounded delivery, not a report discount. Include currency, fee, payment milestones, included costs, separately payable tools/travel/taxes where applicable, and the process for scope changes. Match any existing agreement; do not invent legal terms.

If no price is authorized, keep a clearly labeled internal fee recommendation with its assumptions; mark the client offer's investment unresolved. Internal approval of a quote is distinct from buyer acceptance. Never substitute a buyer's stated budget for an approved fee.

Keep the future build outside this purchase. Do not imply that a build, subscription, ongoing training, or fee credit is included unless explicitly approved. A speculative implementation estimate is not a fixed quote. End with a checkable commercial block or an explicit unresolved list.

### 4. Prepare the post-discovery delivery

Write the concise client-facing offer in `templates/offer.md` and keep source citations, assumptions, qualification, and internal pricing reasoning in a separate internal note. Lead with the buyer's problem and the capability they will leave with, then scope, success measures, investment, responsibilities, and the next decision.

Prepare a short spoken explanation and an unsent follow-up message: connect the discovery pain to a practical learning outcome, explain the bounded purchase, and invite review of the scope and terms. Avoid generic AI hype or implying they must buy implementation to receive value.

The **initial offer** can be shared for review through the user's authorized channel. The **later opportunity map** has a separate live-review workflow in step 5. Do not conflate the two or withhold an offer the buyer needs for purchasing. If external delivery is authorized, verify recipient, final version, and channel; report what actually sent. Otherwise return the draft and proposed next step without contacting anyone.

### 5. Specify the delivery-to-opportunity handoff

Use `templates/delivery-plan.md` to make the sold package executable. During each session, collect the task's inputs/outputs, frequency, owner, current effort and rework, friction, attempted alternatives, and desired change. Observe adoption directly: who completes the task independently, where they struggle, and what support or tool constraint remains. Do not infer these observations from the sales call.

Turn the accumulated evidence into an opportunity map, distinguishing training/SOP improvements, plausible automations needing validation, and work to defer or leave unchanged. Assign each opportunity an evidence source, user owner, expected benefit with assumptions, adoption readiness, dependencies, and next validation step. Separate released capacity from cash savings; do not add overlapping benefits or convert opinions into measured ROI.

Arrange the map review before the final session ends when authorized; otherwise draft the scheduling request and name its owner. Present the map live by default, then share the reviewed version. If procurement, accessibility, or the buyer requires advance materials, agree a preview and record the exception rather than obstructing the sale. Mark proposed, scheduled, and completed meetings distinctly.

### 6. Offer a build only when the evidence supports it

Before the review, a requested possible build handoff is a conditional validation brief, not a selected implementation or finished quote.

At the review, choose among continued self-service, further enablement, a bounded technical validation, or a separately scoped implementation. The client keeps the agreed training materials regardless. Identify the decision-maker and next action without assuming a sale or changing the deal stage.

For an implementation proposal, carry forward the selected workflow evidence, acceptance criteria, end-user owner, dependencies, and adoption plan. Specify the ongoing training cadence, reusable lessons, SOP ownership, and support period in the new scope and price; monthly team sessions are an option, not a free recurring commitment. Leave unvalidated feasibility and implementation cost unresolved rather than disguising them as training findings.

## Output and completion check

Save under the existing deal/client convention; where it uses `02-proposals/`, use dated, deal-specific filenames for the offer, internal rationale, and delivery plan. Later workshop observations belong in the agreed delivery folder, linked back to the offer. Update the canonical record only with actual draft/delivery status, evidence, next action, and blockers.

Return the artifacts for the current stage: steps 1–4 produce the initial offer package below; an existing accepted engagement needs only its rationale/handoff; steps 5–6 produce the map, review plan and any conditional next-scope brief. Do not manufacture a new offer to satisfy this checklist.

1. Fit decision and why; source-backed unknowns that affect it.
2. Client-facing offer plus spoken explanation and unsent follow-up.
3. Delivery plan and initially unfilled opportunity-map structure, unless sessions have already happened.
4. Readiness: **conditional draft**, **ready for operator review**, **approved for delivery**, or **delivered** with evidence. Separate this from **client accepted**, which requires its own evidence.

Before calling an offer ready for delivery, verify that scope, investment, terms, responsibilities, and first step are internally approved; buyer claims are supported; hypothetical workshop results and internal notes are absent from client copy; placeholders are resolved; and no build is silently bundled. A completed draft is not a delivered offer or a validated sales method.

## Anti-patterns

- Renaming an unchanged audit “enablement” without teaching usable skills.
- Replacing a functioning audit offer solely because an article's headline says to.
- Generic workshops without role-specific practice or end-user participation.
- Fabricated fees, results, dates, buyer approval, or guaranteed conversion lifts.
- Selling training as a mandatory deposit toward an unspecified build.
