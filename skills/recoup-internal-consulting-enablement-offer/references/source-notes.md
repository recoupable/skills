## What the source supports

Pierce describes experimenting with paid team enablement alongside audits, not abandoning effective audits. His proposed sequence is a defined training purchase, leadership and departmental sessions using real work, embedded discovery, an opportunity map, a live review arranged during delivery, then an optional implementation offer with continued training and durable learning materials. The training should benefit the team even without a build. He argues that practice exposes adoption barriers and can make the initial purchase more tangible.

This is operator commentary, not comparative sales research. It supplies neither a universal price nor a complete curriculum, and its paid playbook was not accessed. Faster sales and broad buyer preference are hypotheses to test, not established Recoup outcomes.

## Recoup adaptations, not source claims

The skill's fit gate, evidence labels, scoped acceptance criteria, pricing authority checks, separate internal/client artifacts, and delivery-status verification are operational design choices. Session counts, duration, price, tools, and success targets must come from the actual engagement. The advance-material exception is a deliberate adaptation to buyer needs, not an assertion that the source permits it.

To evaluate this offer locally, record qualified opportunities, offer type, dated proposal and acceptance events, declines and reasons, delivery cost, participant task completion, and independently observed reuse. Compare comparable deals; report sample size and confounders. Training revenue and adoption matter independently of later build sales. Do not claim a causal conversion improvement from one win.
