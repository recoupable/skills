# Public content quality gate

Apply this gate to public articles, posts, emails, scripts, cover text, captions, alt text and links. Workspace instructions and explicit user direction take priority over examples in older references.

## 1. Public sources and competitors

- Do not mention, quote, link to or promote Recoup competitors or their shows in public copy. Music Fastball and Music Moneyball are excluded. Read the selected workspace's competitor policy for additional names; do not presume ordinary software suppliers or customers are competitors.
- Keep accurate research provenance in private briefs/manifests. Do not export private source paths or editorial notes with the article.
- Never solve a forbidden attribution by deleting the name and retaining the quotation, distinctive framing, statistic or case study as the author's own. Replace that passage with an original explanation grounded in permissible evidence, or remove it. Label illustrative examples and invented figures at first use. Do not claim the author performed work described by somebody else.
- If the argument depends on excluded evidence, hold the piece for a new angle or permissible source. No euphemisms such as 'an industry podcast' to preserve the same borrowed endorsement.
- Scan every companion format and cover, not only the article. An existing published source is historical evidence; editing it does not update a live site or sent post. Keep publication history separate from proposed revisions.

## 2. First-read clarity

Before drafting, write privately: intended reader; their question; one useful answer; next action. Lead public copy with that question or answer, not a surprising slogan or source introduction.

Use one concrete example through the explanation. Name the actor, input, action and output where relevant. Define necessary terms on first use; otherwise use plain words. Prefer 'income received versus the forecast' to 'hold-period monitoring against underwriting'. Do not silently change cash receipts into earned revenue, missing data into zero, plans into shipped results, or a comparison into a guarantee.

Keep paragraphs short and remove sections that repeat the point. A useful 400–700-word article is preferable to padding for a target. Longer pieces must earn their length. Readability formulas and word counts are triage aids, not editorial approval.

A non-technical reader must be able to explain the point after the opening and identify the next step at the end. If they cannot, rewrite before deriving companion posts. Review quotes, numbers and who said/did what across every format after rewriting.

## Recoup voice: established methods use present tense

Describe Recoup’s established services and working methods in active present tense: “We start”,
“We build”, “We test”, “We train”, “Recoup helps”. Do not make real services sound hypothetical
with “we would”, “we could”, “we’d”, or “Recoup would”.

- Method: “We start with the employee who built the workflow and the person who checks its output.”
- Illustrative example: “For a catalog report, that might mean testing a completed quarter.”
- Verified result: “The team reduced preparation time” only when supported by evidence.

Apply this to articles, posts, emails, headlines, excerpts, captions and CTAs. Review the whole
paragraph: follow-on wording such as “the team could review” or “finance would own” may also need
present-tense wording when describing the agreed method. Prefer “can review” and “finance owns”.

This is an editorial review, not a blanket replacement. Preserve quotations, past events (including
“we’d” meaning “we had”), real conditions, illustrative scenarios and proposals for work not yet offered.
Do not turn planned features, sample outputs or hoped-for savings into shipped capabilities or proven
results. Use “we recommend” for advice rather than inventing a track record. Describe scope-dependent
steps with their condition: “Once the reports pass review, we connect the recurring inputs.”

## 3. Cover comprehension

Draft the cover text before artwork. It must name the subject and what the reader will learn or do without help from the article, caption, filename or surrounding UI. Use roughly 3–8 plain words; never sacrifice meaning to a word limit.

Bad: 'The clock is a choice.' Better: 'Match your fund to your catalog.'
Bad: 'Priced once. Owed every quarter.' Better: 'Track your music catalog income.'
Bad: 'Own the system.' Better: 'Assign someone to manage company AI.'

Show the final cover at full size and around 160px. Ask a fresh reader what they expect the article to teach. Their answer must match the actual article. Text-source inspection is not visual review. Keep the editable source, exported cover, article title, alt text and companion copy consistent. Re-render after text edits; do not mark a source-only change complete.

## 4. Release evidence

Record the reviewed files, reviewer persona, concrete changes, remaining blockers and actual checks in the private manifest. Revisions invalidate earlier gate results for changed material. A 100-point mechanical score, generated image, or Git commit cannot certify clarity, visual quality, publication, or live delivery. Keep drafts staged until explicitly published.

For an existing-library audit, enumerate all public formats first, inspect each, preserve historical evidence, and record one disposition per bundle. Do not use keyword absence as proof that all prose or images passed.
