# Distribution, experiments and learning

## Account-level consistency

Keep the selected identity, account promise and presentation recognizable. A profile/cover should accurately describe the experience the next post delivers. Each episode remains independently understandable; lore and episode numbers must not be prerequisites. Pin strong representative work after results justify it, while keeping pinned posts out of chronological growth comparisons.

Choose cadence from sustainable production and the audience's behavior. Hold it steady while testing creative structure; introduce a second slot only as a declared variable. A common posting hour across several accounts does not prove common ownership or a magic distribution window. Do not “warm” accounts through fake engagement, promise followers, or clone near-identical pages to manufacture chances at a hit.

Publish to each verified destination with its native crop, safe areas, cover, audio rights and available settings. Reusing one's original master across platforms is distinct from stealing/reposting another creator's content; adaptation may improve fit, but do not invent an absolute rule that every cross-post is suppressed. Review current platform eligibility, audience classification and synthetic-media requirements when publishing. Cartoon style alone does not establish the intended age audience or a universal AI-label rule.

The owning workspace's content DB, QA, thumbnail, review and publisher sequence remains authoritative. Bind approval to the final media hash, caption, account and schedule. Store schedule IDs and eventual platform results separately. If one destination fails, reconcile that destination only; do not re-send to successful accounts. Never change disclosure settings or automatic-sharing options as an unrequested growth tactic.

## Three distinct feedback loops

1. **Creative production:** Which scenes render as intended? Track rejected attempts, editing minutes, identity failures and cost per accepted episode.
2. **Audience response:** Which promises, beats and endings hold attention or prompt sharing/return? Track comparable-age native metrics and qualitative responses.
3. **Business result:** Does that audience take the selected next action? Track verified opt-ins, listening, sales or qualified inquiries only when relevant and measurable. Gross views cannot demonstrate conversion or profit.

Keep them separate. High view counts do not repair unsustainable production; a beautiful controllable render does not establish demand.

## Design the test before the batch

Write objective, baseline, changed variable, controls, observation window, spend/labor ceiling, decision rule and stopping conditions. If no baseline exists, call the first cohort baseline-building rather than validation. Match the minimum observation volume to the decision and uncertainty; do not manufacture statistical confidence from a small sample.

The article's thirty-reel suggestion is a heuristic for repeated attempts, not a requirement or statistically validated threshold. Bounded batches let the operator repair craft failures early. Keep identity/format stable but make substantially different episodes. Freeze competing changes when interpreting a result; where multiple things changed, record the comparison as exploratory.

For timing tests, balance comparable topics/formats across times and days; monitor at the same post age. Do not put all stronger episodes in one slot and call the slot causal. For Instagram Trial Reels, verify account availability and actual connector support. Trials are real publication, not private drafts or a randomized experiment. Keep trial and standard-release cohorts separate; preserve any automatic full-sharing setting unless changing it is authorized.

## Measurement contract

One observation row represents a specific **post × platform × timestamp**. Include:

- Account, episode, recipe/reference versions, source-performance and final hashes, platform post ID.
- Publish/schedule/delivery time, timezone, observation time and elapsed age.
- Organic/paid/collaboration/trial/pinned status; duration, format and changed variable.
- Native metric names/definitions and raw values: reach, starts/views, engaged views, watch time, shares/sends, saves, comments, profile visits and attributed follows, as available.
- Evidence source/export timestamp; missing/partial/delayed fields, counter resets or definition changes.

For repeated snapshots, compare posts at a declared checkpoint (for example 24 hours and seven days); never add cumulative snapshots together. If a snapshot misses the target, report its actual age and exclude or stratify it rather than invent an interpolated count. Retain failures/deleted posts in the experiment ledger so only survivors do not define success.

For recurring-audience goals, record the platform-native returning-viewer definition and observation window. Keep account-level return measures separate from post-attributed outcomes; do not assign account-level returns to a particular episode or posting slot without attribution evidence. Use the owner's qualification criteria and available evidence. If return or qualification cannot be observed, leave it unknown: follows and shares are useful proxies, not measurements of either.

### Calculations and interpretation

| Measure | Definition | Limitation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Share/send rate | Named action count / named eligible exposure denominator | Prefer unique reach where available; otherwise label per-view explicitly. Not interchangeable across platforms. |
| Follow conversion | Post-attributed follows / reach, or the documented denominator | Profile-level net change is not post-attributed follows. Shares may create delayed/unattributed visits. |
| Watch proportion | Average watch duration / video duration | Not completion rate; replays may push it above 100%. Native denominators differ. |
| Completion | Native full-view count / documented eligible starts, only if available | Do not reconstruct from average watch time or substitute missing data with zero. |
| Relative performance | Same-age result / median of comparable account/format posts | Baseline zero or absent makes a ratio undefined. External platforms/pages are not a controlled comparison. |
| Hit concentration | Largest post's count / total counts in the same cohort/checkpoint | Describes dependence on one outlier, not a probability that another post will hit. |
| Accepted-output cost | All incurred production costs, including failures/rework, / accepted finals | Report estimated versus billed cost and labor separately; no accepted finals means undefined, not zero. |

Show denominator, sample count, age window and missingness alongside ratios. Keep median per-post rate distinct from pooled actions/exposures; the latter weights large posts more heavily. Do not silently remove outliers: report median, spread, top share and the best post, with the weak posts still visible. Compute within platforms; metric names that look identical may count different behavior.

YouTube's public Shorts view count includes starts/replays under the March 2025 change, whereas engaged views remain separate. Use the current export's definitions; public views, viewed-versus-swiped, engaged views, retention and monetization eligibility are not synonyms. Account for metric-version breaks in historical comparisons.

## Diagnose before prescribing

| Evidence pattern | Investigate | Bounded next test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Weak initial viewing/early retention | First frame, situation clarity, expectation mismatch | Improve entry/comprehension while holding the premise and payoff stable |
| Strong entry, loss before reveal | Repeated beats, unclear cause, excessive delay | Remove a redundant beat or stage the necessary action more clearly |
| Good watch response, low sharing | Recipient relevance/emotional specificity; metric availability | Test a clearer relational framing, not a generic CTA pasted onto the same scene |
| High views, weak follows/return | Account promise, recognizable cast, consistency of the next episode | Clarify continuity and profile promise using the approved identity |
| One huge hit, ordinary remainder | Outlier premise, age, pinning, external boost | Reproduce the underlying mechanism in new episodes; do not merely reupload |
| Good visible metrics, low distribution | Competition, eligibility, sample size, traffic source, reporting delay | Verify those inputs before announcing a shadowban or changing everything |
| Production failures dominate | Reference quality, interaction complexity, adapter reliability | Simplify shots or compare a controllable rendering route under a bounded test |

These are diagnostic hypotheses, not deterministic algorithm explanations. Comments can reveal interpretation and recurring audience language, but commenters are a selected subset. Check negative/confused responses, not only compliments. Do not auto-DM or reply as the owner without authority.

## Promotion and stop conditions

Record **continue / revise / pause / insufficient evidence**, what supports it and what would change it. Promotion requires reproducible accepted production plus audience evidence appropriate to the goal across more than one isolated win. Use predeclared, account-specific thresholds; leave missing thresholds explicit during baseline work rather than invent universal viral benchmarks.

Pause spend when an unresolved job may already have billed, the budget/labor cap is exhausted, identity keeps drifting, the visual payoff cannot be rendered, or the necessary authorization is absent. A failed experiment stays as evidence. After a meaningful change, version the recipe and rerun only the affected checks. Expand account count or automation after the first workflow is repeatable—not to escape learning why it fails.
