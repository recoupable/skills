# Toon account pipeline

Turn observed audience behavior into a repeatable character-led short-form production and learning loop. Optimize for people choosing to watch, sharing with someone, and returning for the characters. Virality is an outcome to investigate, not a guarantee this skill can make.

## Contract and scope

- Explain or operate the pipeline at the user's requested stage. **Research/pipeline-only requests do not authorize inventing account ideas, names, niches, characters, or sample story concepts.** Use the supplied canon, observed references, or neutral placeholders. Missing canon does not block a method-only deliverable.
- Separate directly observed evidence, creator-reported claims, official platform guidance, and working hypotheses. Distinguish a recognizable format from materially repetitive content.
- Keep private account records, analytics, references and run artifacts in the selected authorized workspace, never in this public skill. “Internal” is audience routing, not confidentiality.
- Preserve existing authority: complete authorized research, preparation and execution without asking again. New spend, uploads, account creation, publishing, or schedule changes need the applicable authorization; a research request supplies none of those.

Read the selected workspace's instructions and existing persona/cast, recipes, current status and account bindings. Do not search unrelated private sources. Bundled resource paths below are relative to this skill's installed directory. No other skill, provider, connector or local repository is required to understand the method.

## 1. Establish the operating question

Identify the requested mode: **research**, **prepare a production packet**, **execute an authorized batch**, or **diagnose results**. Record the actual goal: qualified audience, recurring viewers, entertainment reach, music discovery, or a commercial action. Views are not a substitute for every goal.

Read available inputs before asking. Keep unresolved decisions explicit. Do not replace the user's selected cast or format with a fresh brainstorm. Use `templates/pipeline-record.md` for the relevant records, not as a requirement to complete every section on every invocation.

## 2. Reverse-engineer the format, not the surface

Read `references/format-and-production.md` for inspection and production mechanics; read `references/evidence.md` when using source claims. Start from the supplied accounts/posts. Compare high, typical, and weak posts within the same account, then compare independent accounts using similar mechanics. Prefer chronological non-pinned samples; annotate sampling method, observation dates, post ages, paid distribution, collaborations and missing metrics.

Inspect the actual media. Capture the opening state, attention target, action/reaction sequence, reveal, ending and caption function. For motion/audio claims use playback or an available video/audio analysis tool; sampled frames support visual sequencing only. State coverage and access limits. A profile grid or transcript is not a watched video, and visual style does not reveal its production software.

For each apparent mechanism record **observation → plausible viewer reason → alternative explanation → test**. Translate “cute characters work” into specific questions about recognition, relationship dynamics, staging, comprehension, emotional payoff and reasons to send/return. Do not convert a winner-selected article into a forecast for a new account.

**Output:** a sourced format breakdown and a testable pipeline, not unsolicited new characters or a launch plan.

## 3. Lock canon and design the episode before rendering

For production, use the supplied/approved cast and audience promise. Version identity, relationship behavior, environments, props and style separately from episode variation and provider configuration. Validate originals/reference lineage; keep new derived images pending review. Never let repeated generation silently redefine the characters.

Prepare the caption/premise and visible payoff together. Decide what a stranger understands from the first frame, what question remains open, and which action answers it. Write a beat sheet and the smallest shot list that communicates it. Every episode must work without previous lore. Reusing a world and format is useful; swapping text on identical footage is not a new story.

Use the appropriate production path from `references/format-and-production.md`: deterministic 2D/cutout animation, reference-conditioned generative motion, or an edited multi-shot sequence. Do not force all toon accounts through photoreal video generation or assume a single ideal duration.

**Output:** an episode packet with canon versions, observable beats, references, shot actions, caption placement, audio plan, selected production route and acceptance criteria. Mark execution bindings as verified, supplied-unverified, missing or proposed; do not invent asset IDs, renderer commands or approved copy to make the packet appear runnable. For method-only work leave creative fields unfilled.

## 4. Execute bounded production when authorized

Inspect the workspace's working adapters and current provider documentation/catalog. Confirm input compatibility, exact model/alias, reference limits, cost basis and available execution access. A website feature, imported source file or authenticated interactive connector is not a deployed worker. No hard-coded provider wins by default.

Follow the selected workspace's media initialization, save and archive workflow. Keep originals and outputs in approved private storage with hashes; manifests may be portable, credentials and signed links are not. Separate persona, reference, recipe, provider, run and publication IDs.

Generate/animate one readable shot at a time where practical; assemble pacing, captions and exact audio timing in the editor. Log submitted job IDs and terminal outcomes. Reconcile an ambiguous paid job before retrying; retain rejected outputs and actual costs separately from estimates.

Review the whole exported video: cast/prop continuity, readable staging, promised action/payoff, motion defects, crop/text, audio and ending. Frame sheets are a diagnostic aid, not full-playback approval. If playback is unavailable, deliver a review candidate explicitly, not a finished pass. Technical completion and editorial acceptance are different states.

**Output:** reviewed exact final hashes, source lineage, costs and holds—or a precise blocked step with prepared work intact.

## 5. Publish and learn through the owning workflow

Read `references/growth-and-measurement.md`. Preserve the chosen account's positioning, destination bindings and existing calendar. Hand the approved hash/caption to its established publishing workflow, including its QA, thumbnail and approval steps. Do not bypass a content engine with a direct scheduler call. A scheduled request is not verified delivery; store/read back per-platform results and reconcile partial failures before retries.

Use native analytics at declared observation ages. Keep reach, starts, engaged views, watch time and follows distinct. Compare within platform/account/format before combining evidence. Examine the cohort's median and spread as well as its breakout post, and inspect what weaker posts failed to communicate. Prefer one decision-relevant change per experiment; balanced exploratory batches are not randomized causal proof.

Scale only after both production repeatability and meaningful audience response justify it. A reusable workflow can produce original episodes; it should not multiply near-identical pages, promise passive revenue or claim a posting-time hack guarantees distribution.

**Output:** continue/revise/pause decision, evidence, one next test, owner and review point. Stop at the requested scope; do not append account ideas to a research result.

## Return format

1. Requested mode, objective and existing canon/inputs.
2. Evidence inspected and limitations; observed mechanisms versus hypotheses.
3. Current pipeline or prepared episode/run packet and exact implementation status.
4. Bottleneck, measurable next experiment and stop/promotion conditions.
5. Saved record paths, completed actions and remaining inputs—not invented results.

## Anti-patterns

- Brainstorming identities when asked to understand or encode a method.
- Copying another page's characters, assets or episodes instead of learning its mechanics.
- Treating two characters, thirty posts, a universal hour, or an arbitrary retention percentage as a success law.
- Changing cast, hook, duration, posting slot and provider together, then attributing the outcome to one factor.
- Claiming a model/provider made a reference clip without evidence, or that sampled-frame inspection verified motion/audio quality.
- Using a single hit, an approval score or gross views as proof of repeatable growth, monetization or a functioning autonomous service.
