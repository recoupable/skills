# AI project selection

Turn a workflow inventory into decisions the business can act on. Select contained work with a useful output and a credible way to test it. Keep all unresolved prerequisites visible.

## Establish the decision

Work in the selected authorized business workspace, read its instructions, and preserve its entity folders. This public skill contains no client records. Templates ship alongside the skill; bundled paths are relative to its installed directory. Do not search unrelated private workspaces for missing inputs.

Read the agreed business goals, candidate register, existing initiatives, available capacity, budget constraints, and evidence. If these are incomplete, continue with a labeled provisional assessment and ask only for information that changes the decision. Do not invent client commitments.

Distinguish proposed work from running work. Running work needs evidence of actual use and accepted outputs. Identify overlapping initiatives and their access boundaries before recommending consolidation.

## Review each candidate

Record its intended output, business owner, recipient, current goal, and purpose of the next test:

- **Feasibility:** resolve a named uncertainty using representative examples and acceptance checks.
- **Capacity:** improve comparable work with measured total human effort, quality, and a named use for recovered time.
- **Commercial:** test an offer or customer outcome with costs and a comparison chosen before the trial.

Enabling work can support several candidates without a direct revenue return. Name the dependent projects and the access, process, or capability gap it resolves. Do not force an enabling project into a fabricated ROI calculation.

For every candidate check permission to use the inputs, a representative example, an accepted-output standard, an accountable reviewer, a baseline or a plan to collect it, delivery capacity, and support responsibility. Use **confirmed / absent / unknown** with evidence. Show every absent or unknown item; do not average away missing access or ownership with an impact score.

Check whether the same result could come from a simpler process change or ordinary automation. Keep existing useful components. Separate retrieval and drafting from consequential actions such as rights changes, payments, or external publication.

## Make and explain the call

Use a decision appropriate to the actual work: prepare prerequisites, run a bounded test, maintain the current workflow, expand a demonstrated workflow, consolidate duplicate efforts, revise, defer, or retire. A recommendation is not permission to execute it.

Order the next commitments by the user's business goals, strength of evidence, dependency sequence, and team capacity. Explain trade-offs in plain language rather than producing an unexplained composite score. Prefer a contained first test when several options have similar value. Do not impose a universal project count or duration.

For the selected project, use `templates/project-brief.md`. Include the full cost range (setup, internal effort, licenses, training, review, operation, maintenance), cost ceiling, owner, success measure, quality floor, review date, fallback, and continue/revise/stop criteria. An unknown amount remains unknown; it does not become zero.

Before a production recommendation, verify authorized access, acceptance evidence, monitoring, support, and the specific actions the system may take. Do not describe a demonstration or approved plan as a deployed system.

## Deliver

Save a dated decision register and first-project brief in the selected entity's delivery folder. Retain deferred/running work and the reason for each decision, dependencies, evidence references, unresolved questions, and what would change the call. Distinguish proposal, owner-approved scope, built, deployed, and measured states.

Return the proposed first commitment and every prerequisite that remains. Route an evidence-based pilot evaluation to the installed `recoup-internal-consulting-ai-pilot-review` skill if available and requested. Do not start systems, purchase tools, send messages, or change production simply because the plan recommends them.
