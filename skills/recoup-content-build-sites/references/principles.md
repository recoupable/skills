# Interpretation principles

This collection is a research base, not an approved taste model. Study differences between specific examples before turning them into rules.

1. **Give the visitor a concrete verb.** Drawing, placing, steering, ranking, finding and making are easier to understand than a metaphorical invitation. The emotional premise can be rich while the instruction stays simple.
2. **Make the response worth the action.** Patatap's authored sound and motion give taps value. Absurd Trolley Problems makes a binary choice interesting through the scenario and comparison. Neither proves that arbitrary tapping or choices are universally good or bad.
3. **Help a novice make something good.** Townscaper, Silk and Incredibox move difficult craft into the system. Distinct output with coherent defaults can be a better reward than a numerical score.
4. **Supply discoveries.** Space Elevator and the Magritte experience have content worth revealing. A map or mysterious room cannot manufacture that value by itself.
5. **Make contributions consequential.** Gartic Phone and Your Line or Mine change because people participate. A share button is distribution, not the underlying social mechanism.
6. **Personalize honestly.** Listening history, an expressed opinion and a creative choice are different inputs. Do not claim evidence from behavior the system never observed.
7. **Keep the subject connection visible.** The original work, product or community should contribute something specific. Swapping a logo onto a generic game is a different proposition from building a meaningful artist experience.
8. **Account for the invisible production.** Animation, good writing, compatible sounds, tuned physics, data and operations often explain why a simple interface works. Preserve the necessary capability when adapting the pattern.
9. **Allow a satisfying ending.** Completion, insight or a finished creation can be success. Longer sessions and mandatory repetition are not universal goals. Touch for Luck intentionally critiques prolonged attention.
10. **Use examples to sharpen judgment.** References inform a decision; they do not replace it. Selective adaptation should preserve the appeal while creating original work suited to the brief.

These are working design principles. They do not establish that a concept will perform well with a particular audience. Record feedback and observed use before calling a reference or pattern proven for that audience.
