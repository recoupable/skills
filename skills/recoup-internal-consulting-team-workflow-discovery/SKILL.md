# Team workflow discovery

Find the recurring work a team would benefit from changing. Produce a candidate register grounded in recent examples, with missing evidence visible.

## Workspace and inputs

Use the selected authorized business workspace and its `AGENTS.md`. Preserve its client/deal structure. Store client answers and evidence there, never in this public skill. Do not search another private checkout for missing inputs. Templates ship alongside this skill; their paths are relative to its installed directory.

Read the agreed business objective, permitted survey responses, interview notes, and any completed examples provided for this engagement. State which teams and roles are represented and which are missing. Do not infer a whole-company finding from one department.

## Collect useful examples

Use `templates/team-worksheet.md` to prepare questions or structure supplied responses. Customize the task language to the client. Preparing a worksheet does not authorize sending it, contacting employees, or creating a survey in another service.

Before collection, identify who can read individual responses, whether names are optional, retention expectations, and the purpose communicated to participants. Do not call a named survey anonymous. Request process descriptions before sensitive underlying records.

Follow a recent job from request to acceptance. Include its operator, recipient, and someone who handles exceptions. Ask one focused follow-up at a time when interviewing interactively. Look for:

- The trigger, accepted output, and decision it supports.
- Preparation, execution, review, correction, and waiting; separate labor from elapsed time.
- Volume, period, and whether numbers are counted or estimated.
- Required files, access owner, restrictions, and authoritative versions.
- Existing tools, informal workarounds, maintainers, and previous attempts.
- What makes the output unusable, who catches mistakes, and what adoption would require.

Preserve conflicting accounts until evidence resolves them. Tenure, enthusiasm, and job seniority are context, not evidence that a person's account is more correct. Do not rank employees or infer job performance from their answers.

## Synthesize the work

Group responses by the same trigger and accepted output, rather than by tool names. Keep separate jobs separate even when they use the same application. Identify duplicated efforts without assuming they can share permissions or customer data.

For each candidate, record: stable ID; business objective; workflow and recipient; operator and owner; current tools; measured or estimated volume/time with period; rework; quality requirement; access constraints; existing approach; proposed change; evidence references; open questions and the person who can answer them.

Retain exact units. Do not multiply already aggregated team hours by headcount. Do not add elapsed waiting time to labor cost. Avoid double counting time shared by overlapping candidates. Mark a benefit as estimated until the work has been measured.

Consider a process correction, deterministic automation, training, or documentation when it fits the evidence. AI is a design option, not a required recommendation.

## Deliver

Save a dated discovery note and candidate register in the entity's existing discovery or delivery folder. Include a concise coverage note and unresolved disagreements. Use local evidence IDs so client records can be traced without copying sensitive excerpts into public material.

Finish with the smallest evidence-gathering action needed for each promising candidate. If the user also asked for prioritization, pass the register to the installed `recoup-internal-consulting-ai-project-selection` skill when available; otherwise provide the register without inventing rankings.
