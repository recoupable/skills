# Registration modes

The mode is an operation on an exact provider record, not a provider name. A new
registration, ordinary claim, amendment and substantive rights dispute have different
preconditions. Use current official rules through `references/provider-readiness.md`.

## Readiness and initial registration

For readiness-only work, return required fields/documents, evidenced values,
unresolved requirements and the intended provider route. Do not submit.

For a new composition or recording, search existing records first. Establish the
object and party identity, represented interest, applicable right/territory/date,
provider role, mandatory identifiers or documented pending-ID path, and required
authentic consents. A composition does not require an invented recording or artist
entity. Separate a work's writers/publishers from a recording's owner/performers.

Prepare the provider's actual payload/schema. Validate party identifiers and share
basis against its rules; do not apply one society's percentage convention to every
service. Validate each batch member and retain accepted, rejected and pending items
independently. A successful file upload is not confirmation that every item registered.

## Ordinary claim on an existing record

Record the exact existing work/recording ID and observed version. Verify that the
claimant's documented interest/role is eligible for this provider's ordinary process.
Submit the supported share/role against that record rather than creating a duplicate.
An ordinary interest claim is not inherently a dispute.

If current competing claims or an adverse response substantively contest the
requested interest, preserve the facts and provider route for resolution. Do not
reduce others' shares, fabricate consent or repeatedly file equivalent new records.

## Amendment and routine rejection repair

Capture current values and the requested corrected values, with evidence for the
change and affected effective period. Check whether the provider requires an edit,
replacement, amendment form, support case or other documented correction route.

An invalid date or missing required field remains in this workflow: retain the failed
receipt, correct supported data, check scope and retry safely. Missing authentic
consent is a prerequisite gap, not a field to invent. A denied substantive remedy
or competing title dispute warrants a resolution handoff.

Read back the accepted correction. If provider receipt says accepted while the
public record has not updated, record acceptance and pending observation separately.
Preserve prior identifiers/values as history where they explain accounting or matching.

## Recording-to-work matching

Match the actual recording version to the composition using identifiers plus
corroborating writer/title/release evidence. A same-title work by other writers is
not a valid substitute. Support multiple works per recording where appropriate.

Use the exact eligible matching route. First-time matching belongs here. A later
rematch/superseding match preserves the original observation and provider history;
do not erase an adverse result. When the proposed link itself is disputed, stop
that action and produce a resolution packet with competing identity evidence.

## Identifier assignment and repair

Search known registries/account records and inspect supplied identifiers before
requesting assignment. Determine the eligible registrant or issuing agency and the
specific identifier type: recording, work, party or product identifiers are not
interchangeable. Use only an actual authorized issuer/registrant process.

Record requested, assigned and observed identifiers separately. Pending assignments
can remain pending where the provider supports them. Never fabricate an ISRC, ISWC,
ISNI, IPI or UPC/EAN to make a payload pass. Ownership or distributor change alone
does not establish a need for a new recording identifier; verify the actual change
and official rules before requesting one.

## Registry or administrator transfer

Use `templates/registration-transition.csv` with the case and operation manifest.
Establish whether the request changes ownership, administration or collection;
distribution migration and internal file storage are separate operations.

For each conveyed interest, preserve retained interests, signed effective dates,
consent conditions, source/target providers, termination/release documents and
historic collection tails. Do not zero an excluded retained interest.

Prepare the actual provider transfer or amendment payload and disclosure list.
Verify which existing claimants or counterparties will receive supporting documents
under that process before relying on the existing authorization scope.

Track independently:

1. Baseline source and target observations.
2. Prepared/submitted payload and receipt.
3. Source release/termination evidence and effective period.
4. Target acceptance and effective period.
5. Observed records at both sides and unresolved overlaps/gaps.
6. A bounded later accounting comparison for usage periods and collection tails.

Target acceptance cannot close a source-side gap. Provider standards and file formats
do not establish eligibility or transport. A catalogue-transfer standard that excludes
distributor transfers cannot be used for a distributor migration.

## Copyright registration, correction and recordation

Identify the jurisdiction, subject matter, claimant, authorship/ownership assertions,
publication facts and intended legal operation from the supplied evidence. Apply the
current official procedure for initial registration, permitted supplementary correction
or transfer recordation, rather than treating all three as one registry edit.

Prepare the correct current form/deposit/documents and identify costs, attestations,
public disclosures and human-only steps. Do not infer a legal conclusion or execute
a certification for the user. Record application/submission, office correspondence,
decision and observed record separately. If eligibility or the legal effect remains
uncertain, deliver the evidence packet and the precise unresolved question.
