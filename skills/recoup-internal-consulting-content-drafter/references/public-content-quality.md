# Public content quality gate

Apply this gate to public articles, posts, emails, scripts, cover text, captions, alt text and links. Workspace instructions and explicit user direction take priority over examples in older references.

## 1. Public sources and competitors

- Do not mention, quote, link to or promote Recoup competitors or their shows in public copy. Music Fastball and Music Moneyball are excluded. Read the selected workspace's competitor policy for additional names; do not presume ordinary software suppliers or customers are competitors.
- Keep accurate research provenance in private briefs/manifests. Do not export private source paths or editorial notes with the article.
- Never solve a forbidden attribution by deleting the name and retaining the quotation, distinctive framing, statistic or case study as the author's own. Replace that passage with an original explanation grounded in permissible evidence, or remove it. Label illustrative examples and invented figures at first use. Do not claim the author performed work described by somebody else.
- If the argument depends on excluded evidence, hold the piece for a new angle or permissible source. No euphemisms such as 'an industry podcast' to preserve the same borrowed endorsement.
- Scan every companion format and cover, not only the article. An existing published source is historical evidence; editing it does not update a live site or sent post. Keep publication history separate from proposed revisions.

## Client privacy, conversational voice and the agent throughline

These checks apply to every Recoup business article, blog, post, newsletter and companion email.
Apply the same care to titles, excerpts, subjects, captions, alt text and artwork. They are editorial
judgment checks, not keyword requirements or a fixed article template.

### Be specific about the work, careful about the client

A name removed is not a client anonymized. Ask from the client's chair: “Would I recognize this as
my situation, and would I feel exposed?” Check combinations of portfolio or team size, exact sums,
dates, locations, unusual events, job titles, distinctive quotes and software arrangements. Ordinary
details can identify someone when combined. Accurate private information is not automatically fit
for publication. Permission to discuss one detail or case does not authorize other identifying detail.

Keep the detail that explains the task, decision or failure. Remove unnecessary identifying detail;
do not swap in another number or invent a different customer. Prefer the report, source documents,
question or review step over the client's scale. If removing a fact changes the argument, record the
remaining permission/evidence need privately and hold that claim. Preserve the source record.

Synthetic editing example: “A director overseeing 27 regional offices needed a weekly report” can
become “You need the weekly report before the team meeting” when clearly framed as an illustrative
scenario. For an evidenced firsthand story, “A client asked me to help with the weekly report” may
fit. Never invent the firsthand relationship to make the sentence friendlier.

Quotes need the same recognition check. Do not alter a quote while retaining quotation marks;
use an accurate paraphrase or omit it. Review the entire bundle, including text inside images.

### Write as someone talking to the reader

Open with a natural, evidenced situation or a direct reader problem. Avoid case-study narration such
as “a finance lead preparing deliverables” when “You're putting together the monthly report” says
what matters. Use “a client told me” only when the evidence supports that conversation. Identify the
person by their job when that responsibility matters, rather than turning job titles into characters.

Use contractions, ordinary verbs, short connected paragraphs and varied sentence lengths. Read the
whole piece aloud, including headings and transitions. Keep the author's natural phrasing when it
works. Do not add slang, invented dialogue, fake intimacy or repetitive one-line lessons to simulate
personality. A formal term may stay when it is the clearest accurate term; explain it if needed.

### Carry the agent's work through the argument

Recoup business thought leadership should help the reader understand how AI agents change the work
being discussed. Establish the connection early, develop it in the example, and make the result or
next decision follow from that work. Mentioning “AI agents” once or appending a generic AI paragraph
does not satisfy this check. Do not relabel every software tool as an autonomous agent.

Privately identify the agent's input, concrete action and useful output. Explain these naturally in
the copy, at the detail the format needs. The reader should be able to say what work the agent does
and what still belongs to a person. For a strategic piece, connect the decision to a specific agent
workflow without turning the article into an instruction manual.

Where an agent's output informs a consequential decision or goes to someone else, explain the
relevant review point: what triggers it, who checks what, what remains unresolved or happens next,
and why that check matters. For example, in an illustrative reporting workflow, an unexplained
change goes to the person who can check its cause before an explanation enters the report. The
benefit is traceability of the explanation, not a claim that all errors are prevented.

Do not bolt human approval onto every low-risk action or repeat the same paragraph across articles.
A brainstorming or formatting example may need no approval step; explain the actual human choice
when relevant. A piece about an adjacent subject needs a useful connection to agent work, not a
fabricated implementation. A format with no relevant agent connection should be marked explicitly
out of scope or returned for a new angle, rather than padded with agent buzzwords.

Keep evidence states distinct: a confirmed implementation describes what we set up; an established
method describes what we do; a recommendation says what to do; an example is visibly illustrative.
An implementation confirmation applies only to that case. Never turn a proposal into shipped work,
invent an automated handoff or threshold, or claim measured savings/accuracy from a design rationale.
Describe why a check makes a report easier to inspect; claim measured improvements only with evidence.

### Verify every format independently

An email or social post must carry its own useful agent connection and context; the linked article
cannot supply missing meaning. Reconcile privacy, voice, mechanism and evidence state across the
article, companions, metadata and figures. Do not force identical wording or identical openings.

The private review record must state for each file: keep/revise/hold; recognition risks removed or
retained with a reason; a conversational-voice finding; the agent's actual task; the relevant human
review/decision and its purpose (or why no review step applies); and any unverified implementation or
outcome. Quote a concrete passage as evidence. A keyword scan or mechanical score cannot certify these
judgments. Revision invalidates approval for the changed version; keep publication history intact.

## 2. First-read clarity

Before drafting, write privately: intended reader; their question; one useful answer; next action. Lead public copy with that question or answer, not a surprising slogan or source introduction.

Use one concrete example through the explanation. Name the actor, input, action and output where relevant. Define necessary terms on first use; otherwise use plain words. Prefer 'income received versus the forecast' to 'hold-period monitoring against underwriting'. Do not silently change cash receipts into earned revenue, missing data into zero, plans into shipped results, or a comparison into a guarantee.

Keep paragraphs short and remove sections that repeat the point. A useful 400–700-word article is preferable to padding for a target. Longer pieces must earn their length. Readability formulas and word counts are triage aids, not editorial approval.

A non-technical reader must be able to explain the point after the opening and identify the next step at the end. If they cannot, rewrite before deriving companion posts. Review quotes, numbers and who said/did what across every format after rewriting.

### Recoup voice: established methods use present tense

Describe Recoup’s established services and working methods in active present tense: “We start”,
“We build”, “We test”, “We train”, “Recoup helps”. Do not make real services sound hypothetical
with “we would”, “we could”, “we’d”, or “Recoup would”.

- Method: “We start with the employee who built the workflow and the person who checks its output.”
- Illustrative example: “For a catalog report, that might mean testing a completed quarter.”
- Verified result: “The team reduced preparation time” only when supported by evidence.

Apply this to articles, posts, emails, headlines, excerpts, captions and CTAs. Review the whole
paragraph: follow-on wording such as “the team could review” or “finance would own” may also need
present-tense wording when describing the agreed method. Prefer “can review” and “finance owns”.

This is an editorial review, not a blanket replacement. Preserve quotations, past events (including
“we’d” meaning “we had”), real conditions, illustrative scenarios and proposals for work not yet offered.
Do not turn planned features, sample outputs or hoped-for savings into shipped capabilities or proven
results. Use “we recommend” for advice rather than inventing a track record. Describe scope-dependent
steps with their condition: “Once the reports pass review, we connect the recurring inputs.”

## 3. Cover comprehension

Draft the cover text before artwork. It must name the subject and what the reader will learn or do without help from the article, caption, filename or surrounding UI. Use roughly 3–8 plain words; never sacrifice meaning to a word limit.

Bad: 'The clock is a choice.' Better: 'Match your fund to your catalog.'
Bad: 'Priced once. Owed every quarter.' Better: 'Track your music catalog income.'
Bad: 'Own the system.' Better: 'Assign someone to manage company AI.'

Show the final cover at full size and around 160px. Ask a fresh reader what they expect the article to teach. Their answer must match the actual article. Text-source inspection is not visual review. Keep the editable source, exported cover, article title, alt text and companion copy consistent. Re-render after text edits; do not mark a source-only change complete.

## 4. Release evidence

Record the reviewed files, reviewer persona, concrete changes, remaining blockers and actual checks in the private manifest. Revisions invalidate earlier gate results for changed material. A 100-point mechanical score, generated image, or Git commit cannot certify clarity, visual quality, publication, or live delivery. Keep drafts staged until explicitly published.

For an existing-library audit, enumerate all public formats first, inspect each, preserve historical evidence, and record one disposition per bundle. Do not use keyword absence as proof that all prose or images passed.
