# Thumbnails and carousels that explain the work

Readers may skim only the large text. Make that text tell the useful story before relying on
captions, footnotes or artwork. Preserve source limits: a documented skill is not a verified
customer result, and a free skill does not make its AI client, app or API usage free.

## Write the headline story first

For a thumbnail, identify the concrete task or benefit and the AI/agent mechanism in the hook.
“Organize your files” could describe manual work; “Let an AI agent organize your files” identifies
who does it, provided the source actually supports that action. This is a synthetic wording example,
not a product claim. Prefer short familiar words and large editable type over a cryptic short slogan.

For a carousel, draft only the headlines, then read them in order. The sequence should make clear:
what problem the agent addresses; what the person supplies; what the agent does and returns;
what evidence or limits matter; what the person decides; and how to take the next step.
Combine or separate these ideas as needed rather than forcing a fixed slide count.

Name AI or the agent early. Later pronouns are fine when their antecedent is clear; repeating
“AI agent” on every card adds noise. Avoid generic sequences such as “Start here / See the gaps /
Take action,” which conceal the mechanism. Keep human approval or judgment explicit where required.
Do not imply that preparing a report executes corrections, sends messages or changes source records.

The final headline should identify the actual offer, such as getting a free agent skill. Add a
conversational invitation and readable website on the closing card. Preserve the campaign's
website invitation in its company and founder captions too. Keep necessary scope limits readable.

## One concept and one visible action per card

Use one simple focal scene that explains the headline at a glance. Prefer a visible action or
relationship: files converge, two records differ, a finding connects to its source, or a person
receives the next task. A lone folder or pencil often names the topic without explaining the work.
Avoid dense tables, dashboard imitations, multiple competing concepts and paragraphs inside images.
Use short labels only when they disambiguate the scene. Keep supporting text to one short thought
where possible; remove repetition rather than shrinking it. These are comprehension choices, not
rigid word limits. Do not replace essential conditions with tiny disclaimers.

## Reuse the cover; illustrate the supporting narrative

For card 1, reuse the existing thumbnail's focal artwork. Crop away its old headline space and
reposition the artwork for the new aspect ratio. Sample or preserve its background color and
compose on one continuous matching canvas. Remove or blend the artwork's source background when
necessary. Do not inset a blue thumbnail rectangle in a green card, leave its old empty text area,
or mistake preserving artwork for preserving an unclear headline. Check edges and halos.

For subsequent cards, generate distinct, simple Higgsfield scenes for the narrative using the
approved style and the skill's cost/provenance steps. Reuse suitable supporting art that already
exists. Cover reuse does not mean repeating the cover object across all cards, and a copy-only
revision does not call for fresh generation. Keep generation receipts and selected-art hashes.

Generate text-free, logo-free artwork. Overlay exact editable DM Sans and the official supplied
Recoup logo. Use the logo on the cover and closing card; intermediate scenes need not repeat it.
Follow the source artwork's appropriate brand background, not a hard-coded blue for every campaign.

## Review the actual exports

Before approving either format:

- Hide body text and captions. A fresh reader of the headlines can explain the agent's job,
  inputs, output and human decision. For a single thumbnail, its task and mechanism are clear.
- Hide the platform caption. Each slide still communicates its one idea and the full sequence
  still makes sense. Check at about 360px per card and the thumbnail at about 160px wide.
- Aim for understanding the focal idea in roughly three seconds. Label this as a design target
  unless a real timed reader test was conducted; author or independent visual review is not that test.
- Inspect final images/PDF for readable type, loaded art, clipping, collisions, seam-free composition,
  correct logo, ordering and consistent page size. A renderer success is not visual verification.
- Independently edit headline sequence, body, labels, alt text and captions against the source.
  After changes, refresh affected exports, transcript, alt text, preview data, review state and hashes.
  Preserve suitable art and unrelated scheduled payloads. Scheduled is not published.

Record the exact reviewed revision, any unresolved problem and the reviewer’s scope. Keep platform
captions as useful additions; they must not rescue an otherwise unintelligible carousel.
